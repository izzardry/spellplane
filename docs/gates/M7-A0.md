# M7-A0 Safari performance

Branch `m7-a0`. Target (PLAN Milestone 7, principles): at least 50 fps in Safari on the owner's Mac in every theme at a
four-player table, 60 fps in Chrome, a smooth iPad, no frame over 50 ms while dragging.

## Tools

- **Frame meter.** `?fps` (every build, production included) or Settings > Display and sound > "Show frame rate". Lower
  left corner, never over the hand, no pointer events, updated four times a second as one text node. Lines:
  1. `58 fps · 16.7 / 18.2 ms`: drawn frames per second and frame time p50 / p95 over the last 2 s, or `idle · 60 Hz`
     when nothing was drawn for 0.5 s (the loop runs on demand);
  2. `max 33 ms · draw 4.1 / 6.0`: the longest gap between two animation frames (stalls show here even when nothing
     was drawn), and the scene's draw time p50 / p95 (`gpu` instead of `draw` with `?render=sync`);
  3. `5120×2880 @2.00 (×0.81) dpr 2.00`: the canvas's drawing buffer, the pixel ratio it renders at, the adaptive
     render scale when below 1, and the display's own ratio;
  4. `dusk · high · post · shadows`: theme, quality level, post-processing, shadows.
  A frame's time runs from the animation-frame callback that drew it to the next one, which the browser holds back
  until the frame is on its way (GPU work included) — in a real browser window. Scripts read the same numbers from
  `__spellplane.fps` (`snapshot()`, `begin()` / `end()` for a phase) or `window.__spellplaneFps` while the meter shows.
- **`pnpm perf`** (scripts/perf/measure.mjs; options in its header). Serves the test build with recorded card data and
  a self-driving scenario: a four-player table, 21 cards on each battlefield (some tapped), seven in the own hand,
  placeholder faces; per theme 3 s idle, 3 s card drag (pointer events every animation frame), 3 s camera pan
  (two-finger scroll every frame). Playwright WebKit and Chromium under pw-guard; `--safari` through safaridriver;
  `--safari-open` in the running Safari without automation; `--variants` for several `?render=` sets in one run.
- **`?render=` switches** (scene/switches.ts): `noadapt`, `nostill`, `shadowall` turn a remedy off; `sync` waits for the
  GPU after each frame; `aa` / `noaa`; `pr=<n>` caps the pixel ratio. Every build, so they can be tried in Safari.

## Where the numbers come from, and what they are worth

All runs on the owner's Mac (Apple M3 Max; a 27-inch Studio Display at 5120 × 2880), with other agents' test runs and
the owner's own work going on (load average 15–40). The scenario's window is 2560 × 1440 CSS px at device pixel ratio
2, a full window on that display: **5120 × 2880 = 14.7 million pixels per frame**.

- **Real Safari: no numbers yet.** safaridriver needs "Allow Remote Automation" (not enabled: `Could not create a
  session: The Safari instance terminated while trying to pair with it`). `--safari-open` works without it, but the
  owner was using Safari at the time and the scenario tab went to the background, where Safari gives a page no
  animation frames; it was not tried again so as not to take over the owner's browser.
- **Headless WebKit and Chromium do not hold animation frames back for the GPU.** Both read about 60 fps in every theme
  at 5120 × 2880, before and after. So they are measured with `render=sync` (a 1-pixel `readPixels` after each frame),
  and the column that matters is **gpu**: the time to draw a frame including the GPU's work, in ms (p50 / p95). WebKit
  rounds its clock to 1 ms. These are relative numbers on a fast GPU: before against after, not Safari.
- "Before" is measured two ways: the build before any change (meter only), and the final build with every remedy
  switched off (`render=noadapt,nostill,shadowall`), which restores the old behaviour; where the two can be compared
  they agree (Dusk draws 60 frames a second at rest in both).

## Causes found, one by one

Measured where a number could be had; "relative" numbers are headless WebKit's GPU time at 5120 × 2880 on the M3 Max.

| Suspect | Finding | Measured cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full Retina pixel ratio | The one lever that scales a lit frame: the world, shadows and the whole post chain run per device pixel. A full window on the 5K display is 14.7 Mpx. | Lit themes 10–12 ms GPU at ratio 2 against 5–6 ms at ratio 1 (−45 to −50 %); Paper 3–4 ms either way. SwiftShader: lit drag 224–396 ms → 80–129 ms. |
| Rendering while nothing changes | Paper and (since M4) every change-driven frame were on demand already; but every lit theme drew every animation frame, including Dusk, where nothing moves at all. Wyrm (embers, seams, shimmer) and Stonetable (motes, rune breath, torch flicker) do animate. | Dusk at rest: 60 frames/s × 10 ms (WebKit) / 13 ms (Chromium) GPU, i.e. 60–80 % of the GPU, for an unchanging picture → 0. |
| Post-processing (bloom, vignette, Wyrm's shimmer) | Half-float targets at the full ratio; MSAA in the chain is already 0 at ratio 2 on large windows (budget in post.ts). Its cost is inside the lit-theme numbers and falls with the pixel ratio. | Lit 10–12 ms vs Paper 3–4 ms at the same size: roughly 7 ms of world shading + post per frame. |
| Shadow maps | Redrawn on every frame, including ambient-only ones where no caster moves. | Within noise on this GPU (≤ 1–2 ms p50 on Wyrm and Stonetable at rest); kept, since it is free. |
| Canvas MSAA (antialias) | Only the overlay (faces, text, lines) is drawn into the multisampled canvas; the lit world goes through the chain's own target. | Paper about 1 ms; lit themes none measurable. Kept on (card edges and lines stay smooth). |
| Texture sizes and mip-maps for faces | Tiers by on-screen CSS height (not by pixel ratio), one texture array per tier, mip-maps regenerated once per upload batch. | No cost at rest; uploads show as gaps while panning to new cards (max 41–50 ms in a few pan phases, before and after). Not changed. |
| troika text | Synchronised only when labels change (`synccomplete` marks the frame dirty). | None at rest; not changed. |
| CSS effects over the canvas | No `backdrop-filter`, `filter: blur` or `will-change` anywhere in the HUD; box shadows are small, on panels only. | Nothing to remove. |
| Per-frame DOM writes | The scene's DOM overlays (focus ring, stack badge, captions, hold ring) write only on change. The **presence layer** (other players' cursors and drags at a networked table) is a full-window 2D canvas that cleared and repainted every animation frame for 30 s after any remote pointer movement — at a four-player table, all the time. | Not in the solo scenario, so not measured; at 5120 × 2880 that was a 59 MB 2D layer redrawn and recomposited 60 times a second in Safari. Now repaints only changed boxes, only when a shape moved. |
| Svelte state per frame | The HUD gets scene events only on change (hover, selection, menus); the meter updates 4 times a second. | None. |
| WebKit WebGL slow paths | No `preserveDrawingBuffer`, no `readPixels` (except the measuring switch), no `finish`; half-float render targets for post (needed for bloom). | Nothing found that headless WebKit shows as slow. |


## What changed

- **Frame meter** (`?fps`, Settings "Show frame rate", `__spellplane.fps`).
- **`pnpm perf`** with Playwright WebKit and Chromium (under pw-guard), safaridriver, and `--safari-open`.
- **Still lit themes draw on demand.** Dusk renders nothing at rest. Wyrm and Stonetable still draw ambient frames
  (their embers, motes, seams and rune glow move), as before.
- **Shadow maps only on change frames.**
- **Adaptive resolution.** While frames run slower than 48 fps the pixel ratio steps down (×0.8, in 1/8 steps) to at
  least one device pixel per CSS pixel — the sharpness of a standard display, so text and faces stay readable — and
  back up after 4 s at 60 fps. A still table is redrawn at the full ratio 1 s after it stops changing, so what the
  owner looks at is always crisp. Card faces are only resampled, never cropped, recoloured or distorted (the M4
  conformance tests check this).
- **Presence layer repaints only what moved.**
- No default quality change for WebKit (see below), no CSS change (nothing costly found).


## What remains

- **Real Safari numbers.** Everything above is headless or software-rendered. Headless WebKit on this GPU is not slow
  at all, so the owner's under-20-fps in Safari comes from what headless WebKit does not do: presenting a 14.7 Mpx
  canvas to a real display with the page composited over it, real card images, or the presence layer at a networked
  table. The meter now shows which, and the adaptive resolution reacts to real frame times, which in a real window
  include those costs. Needed: one `pnpm perf -- --safari-open` (or `--safari`) run, and the meter's numbers from a
  real four-player game in each theme.
- **First frames of a run at a new ratio** pay for the new canvas and post buffers (max up to about 300 ms on
  SwiftShader, 40–50 ms on WebKit at 5K). On a slow machine that is one hitch when a drag starts after the table has
  been still for over a second; if Safari shows it, the next step is to keep the canvas at full size and scale only the
  post chain's targets (faces stay at the full ratio).
- **A WebKit default quality level** needs `Settings.quality` to tell "never chosen" from "high" (store/types.ts, a
  contract file); not done, and on this evidence (shadows within noise) it would buy little over the adaptive ratio.
- **Contract follow-ups** for the next contracts package: `ThemeInstance.animating?()` (now `StillAware` in
  scene/theme-runtime.ts) and a `showFps` setting (now localStorage).
- **Real card images** (`--faces scryfall`) were not measured.
- **The iPad** was not measured; it gets the same adaptive ratio (ratio 2, floor 1).

  14 cores) to drop.
