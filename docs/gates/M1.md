## Hidden information and protocol: accepted residual risks

These are recorded in `docs/DECISIONS.md` under M1, each with its reason. Please review them:
- **The sequencer is trusted for order.** The sequencer can mark a peer away and then clear that peer's chair before a shuffle commit. The log shows it, but the reducer cannot refuse it, because actions are unsigned (DESIGN "Handshake"). A non-sequencer cannot clear another seat's chair unless that seat is away (`checkSeatLeave`).
- **A sequencer that changes a peer's nonce or commit** is caught by the session, which compares the stamped copy with its own pending copy and posts an integrity warning in the log.
- **A chair taken during an open shuffle or dice round** is not refused. Peer nonces travel in the clear by design, so refusing would not close anything.
- **Away seats are still waited for** in shuffle and dice rounds. Otherwise the 1v1 sequencer could decide a round alone by marking the other seat away.
- **Re-issued hand ids** are checked only by the post-game audit.
- **Losing the key pair as well as the save:** lost-save recovery needs the same browser identity (key pair) to take the chair back. A player who cleared all site data or switched devices cannot rejoin their chair in M1. The design names exactly these two cases, so this is a gap to decide.
- **Reveals skipped by a snapshot catch-up** (more than 500 actions behind) carry no salts. Such a card cannot be named again after a lost save.

The independent review of M1-P8 approved its last fix. Its minor findings (not leaks):
1. **Turn-up lost after a long absence.** A face-up play sequenced while the player was away is lost when that player catches up from a snapshot: the card stays face down, and the owner can flip it by hand.
2. **Undo is not checked.** The rule for a kept turn-up ignores `undo`. The outcome agrees with DESIGN (a reveal cannot be undone), but the `[M1-G fix 2]` decision line overstates what is checked.
3. **Stale view after a reload.** An own action taken while the page is still catching up can drop a turn-up that was owed.

The reviewer's scratch reproductions are kept outside the repository.

## Infrastructure decisions made during M1

- **Identity keys are stored as JWK, not as CryptoKey objects.** WebKit protects stored CryptoKeys with a macOS keychain item, and that raised keychain dialogs on every test run.
- **Playwright guard.** Every Playwright run goes through `scripts/pw-guard.mjs`:
  - one run per machine, across all worktrees;
  - leftovers of a dead run are killed;
  - the run aborts (exit 3) when macOS shows a security or privacy prompt, and aborts (exit 4) when memory runs low;
  - locally: 2 workers, a limit of 10 failures, a 45-minute cap.

  This followed two machine crashes caused by many parallel browser runs.
- **Review severity rule.** A review finding blocks a merge only if it is reproduced, contradicts the design, or breaks a hard rule. Everything else goes into the gate report. This is now the default for M2–M5.
- **Workflow resume mode** (`resume: true`), so packages survive a crash. Workflow arguments are kept outside `/tmp`.

## Owner checks

1. **Two-home network check.** Open the live URL on two machines in different homes. Create a table and share the link. Both join and Ready a real deck. Play a few turns, roll a die, and reload one side. Also run `pnpm e2e:network` (the `@network` smoke test against public Trystero signalling) and open `/#/diag` on both sides.
2. **Lost-save recovery.** In one browser, clear the site data for the table's room only. Keep the identity: clearing all site data also removes the key pair, which is the known gap above. Then reopen the table link, re-import the deck in the recovery panel, and agree on the other side.

## Owner playtest checklist

1. Play a 1v1 across two browsers (or two machines):
   - **Lobby:** chairs, seat colour, Ready.
   - **Mulligans.**
   - **Stack:** cast and target with a right-drag; resolve with R; counter with Shift+R.
   - **Holds:** hold with H, and check that the confirmation prompt's timer feels right.
   - **Combat:** attack, double block, settle.
   - **Hidden cards:** play a face-down card and reveal it to the opponent; peek and scry.
   - **Dice:** Ctrl+I.
   - **Chat:** use `[[card name]]` previews.
2. Close the sequencer's tab mid-game. Check that the other side sees the away seat, then reopen the tab.
3. Judge the log wording (see the minor findings) and the confirmation prompt's position.
4. Check the connectivity-failure message on a restrictive network (or with signalling blocked).
