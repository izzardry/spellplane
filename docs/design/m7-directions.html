Spellplane M7: three visual directions

The same two screens in three looks: the 1v1 table at the moment "my turn, main phase, a few creatures and lands down, 7 cards in hand", and the start page. Each direction also has an iPad portrait thumbnail of the table.

The structure is the same in all three: life where the player sits, one turn tracker, one primary button, Undo, a menu for rare tools, the log collapsed to a button, and card actions on selection. That structure is in hud-proposal.md, so you can judge it separately from the style. Here, judge the mood, colour, type and materials.

The cards are neutral placeholders with black borders, roughly how most real Scryfall images sit on the table. Real card faces are never tinted or cropped. Nothing on this page loads from the network.

Direction AKitchen Table

Warm and tactile: an oatmeal playmat on walnut, cream paper chips, rounded friendly type.

Like the evenings it is for: a wooden table, a cloth playmat, a pad for life totals and a bowl of dice. Everything the HUD shows looks like a physical object you could pick up — cream paper chips with soft, short shadows, round tokens for the players, one brick-red accent for the thing to do next. The table surface is a light oatmeal linen, so black-bordered cards sit on it with the most contrast of the three; the wood only frames the edges. Calm, homely, a little playful, never cartoonish.

Direction BGraphite

Modern and minimal: a cool charcoal surface, flat hairline panels, one mint accent, precise type.

A precise instrument that gets out of the way. The surface is a deep cool charcoal with a barely visible dot grid; territories are separated by a single hairline. HUD panels are flat, with a 1-pixel border and no shadow, so the cards — the only bright, saturated things on screen — carry all the colour. One mint accent marks the next action and the selection; everything else is greys. It reads like a well-made app, calm at 1 a.m., and it is the cheapest of the three to render.

Direction CCodex

Restrained fantasy: midnight-blue leather, gilt hairlines, serif small caps, a vermilion seal for the next action.

A spellbook on a library desk, not a dragon's lair. The table is a deep midnight-blue, faintly tooled with a geometric knot pattern and framed by a thin gilt line with diamond corners. Panels are ink-dark with a hairline of gold; headings are in serif small caps, body text stays a clean sans for legibility. The one warm accent is a vermilion "seal" on the primary action. No particles, no glow, no perspective — the fantasy lives in materials and type, so it stays quiet during play.

You can mix them: for example "B's flat panels with A's warm surface", or "C, but with A's rounded type". Whichever you pick becomes the design-system tokens (colour, type, spacing, radius, elevation, icons, motion). The four table themes then change materials and mood on top of it, never the layout or the controls.
